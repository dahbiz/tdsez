Potential & Mass

The static part of the Hamiltonian is defined by three inputs: the potential \(V(\mathbf r)\), the position-dependent effective mass \(m(\mathbf r)\) and the carrier charge \(q\). Potential and mass are muParser expressions of the coordinates; all quantities are in atomic units (see Physics Conventions).

Hamiltonian

The field-free Hamiltonian uses the BenDaniel-Duke form of the kinetic operator [BenDanielDuke1966], which is Hermitian for a position-dependent mass:

(22)\[H_0 = -\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{m(\mathbf r)}\,\nabla\right) + s\,V(\mathbf r), \qquad s = -q .\]

In the Galerkin discretisation with basis functions \(B_a\) (see Grid & Domain) the operators of Eq. (22) become

(23)\[K_{ab} = \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{\nabla B_a\cdot\nabla B_b}{m(\mathbf r)}\,d\mathbf r, \qquad V_{ab} = s\int V(\mathbf r)\,B_a B_b\,d\mathbf r, \qquad M_{ab} = \int B_a B_b\,d\mathbf r ,\]

with \(H_0 = K + V\) and \(M\) the overlap matrix. The weak form of the kinetic term needs only first derivatives, so the BenDaniel-Duke matching conditions at a mass discontinuity (continuity of \(\psi\) and of \(m^{-1}\,\partial_n\psi\)) are satisfied naturally; KnotSequence = interface places C0 knots at such discontinuities (Knot Sequences). \(m(\mathbf r)\) and \(V(\mathbf r)\) are evaluated at every quadrature point.

Carrier Charge

Key

Type

Default

Description

Charge / ParticleCharge

real

-1.0

Carrier charge \(q\) in units of \(e\). -1 is an electron, +1 a hole or another positive carrier.

Potential and the laser field are written for an electron. The carrier sign \(s = -q\) multiplies both the potential and the field term (Laser Field): for \(q = -1\) they enter as written, for \(q = +1\) both are inverted, and a general \(q\) scales them. A positive Charge prints a note at start-up. The reported dipole, current and acceleration are multiplied by \(q\) (Physics Conventions).

Potential

Key

Type

Default

Description

Potential / ExternalPotential

muParser

required

\(V(x,y,z)\) in Hartree. The expression is evaluated at sample points inside the box before assembly; a parse error or a non-finite value stops the run.

PotentialDerivativeX / Y / Z

muParser

none

Analytic \(\partial V/\partial x\), \(\partial V/\partial y\), \(\partial V/\partial z\). Required on every axis of the problem when EnablePropagation = 1 (write 0 for a constant potential); they enter the force operator of the dipole acceleration (Observables). They are not computed from Potential, and a wrong derivative gives a wrong acceleration.

Example (1D soft-core atom, from examples/hhg/hhg_1d.inp):

Potential            = -1.0 / sqrt(x*x + 1.0)
PotentialDerivativeX = x / (x*x + 1.0)^1.5

Mass

Key

Type

Default

Description

Mass

muParser

1.0

Effective mass \(m(x,y,z)\) in units of \(m_e\). It must be positive and finite; it is checked at sample points inside the open box (16 per axis), so a singularity exactly on a Dirichlet wall is admissible.

MassIsConstant

bool

0 (detected)

Set to 1 automatically when the Mass expression contains none of x, y, z. A constant mass skips the finite-difference derivatives of \(1/m\) in the force operator and selects the operator form of the acceleration with AccelerationMethod = auto. Setting it to 1 for a position-dependent mass drops these gradient terms; the kinetic, velocity and \(1/m\) matrices always evaluate \(m(\mathbf r)\) at each quadrature point.

Example (Quesne oscillator, position-dependent mass on \(x > -2\)):

Mass                 = (1.0 + x/2.0)^(-1.0)
Potential            = x*x/(4.0*(x + 2.0))
PotentialDerivativeX = x*(x + 4.0)/(4.0*(x + 2.0)*(x + 2.0))

Expressions and Constants

All expressions use muParser syntax. Potential, PotentialDerivativeX/Y/Z and Mass are functions of x, y, z (unused coordinates are 0 in 1D and 2D); the field expressions are functions of t only (Laser Field). In the spatial expressions fabs( is accepted as a synonym of abs(. The constant pi is defined in the potential, derivative and field expressions; in Mass use muParser’s built-in _pi.

Key

Type

Description

Variables

string

User-defined constants as comma-separated Name=value pairs, for example Variables = V0=1.2, w=0.5. Values are numbers, not expressions. Several Variables lines accumulate (it is the only key that may be repeated). The constants are available in every expression: Potential, PotentialDerivativeX/Y/Z, Mass and the field expressions.

The following input parameters are also defined as constants in every expression, with their parsed values:

Q and Charge, Dimension, SplineDegree, Nelements and NSplines, LMinX, LMaxX, LMinY, LMaxY, LMinZ, LMaxZ, OffsetX, OffsetY, OffsetZ, Ampx, Ampy, Ampz, CEPx, CEPy, CEPz, Omegax, Omegay, Omegaz, TargetEigenvalue, TimeStep, FinalTime, CAPKmin, KnotAlpha, NQuadratures, NBoundStates.

Other keys are not available as constants; define the values needed with Variables.
