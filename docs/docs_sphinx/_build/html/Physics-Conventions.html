Physics Conventions

Units, sign conventions, the discrete equations of motion and the operators used throughout TDSE-Z.

Units

All input and output quantities are in atomic units: energy in Hartree, length in Bohr (\(a_0\)), time in \(\hbar/E_h\), mass in electron masses \(m_e\), charge in elementary charges \(e\), and \(\hbar = 1\). The field strength is in \(E_h/(e a_0)\).

Hamiltonian and Carrier Sign

The time-dependent Schrödinger equation is solved in the length gauge:

(28)\[i\,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \left[-\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{m(\mathbf r)}\,\nabla\right) + s\,V(\mathbf r) + s\,\mathbf E(t)\cdot\mathbf r - i\,W(\mathbf r)\right]\psi, \qquad s = -q .\]

  • The kinetic term has the BenDaniel-Duke form [BenDanielDuke1966] with the effective mass Mass (Potential & Mass).

  • \(V\) is Potential and \(\mathbf E(t)\) the field of LaserX/Y/Z (or Laser) on the polarised axes, zero on the others (Laser Field).

  • \(q\) is Charge in units of \(e\) (default \(-1\), electron). Potential and field are written for an electron: for \(q = -1\) (\(s = 1\)) they enter as written and the force of the field is \(-\mathbf E\); for \(q = +1\) (hole) both are inverted, \(H = K - (V + \mathbf r\cdot\mathbf E)\); a general \(q\) scales both, \(H = K - q\,(V + \mathbf r\cdot\mathbf E)\).

  • \(W \ge 0\) is the complex absorbing potential [Manolopoulos2002], present only with EnableCAP = 1 and only during the propagation; the eigensolver uses the Hermitian \(H_0\) (Boundary Conditions).

Discrete Equations

With \(\psi(\mathbf r, t) = \sum_j c_j(t)\,B_j(\mathbf r)\) in the B-spline basis [deBoor2001] [Bachau2001], assembled with PetIGA [Dalcin2016] [Hughes2005], the Galerkin equations are

(29)\[i\,M\,\dot{\mathbf c} = \Big(H + s \sum_{a\in P} E_a(t)\,D_a\Big)\,\mathbf c ,\]

with the overlap matrix \(M\), \(H = K + V - iW\) (matrices of Eq. (23), \(W\) only with the CAP) and the dipole matrices \(D_a\). The Crank-Nicolson propagator integrates the residual form

(30)\[F(t, \mathbf c, \dot{\mathbf c}) = i\,M\,\dot{\mathbf c} - H\,\mathbf c - s \sum_{a\in P} E_a(t)\,D_a\,\mathbf c = 0 .\]

Operators

Quantity

Matrix

Overlap

\(M_{ij} = \int B_i B_j\,d\mathbf r\). It defines the inner product \(\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = \mathbf a^\dagger M\,\mathbf c\) used for all norms, projections and populations.

Dipole (position)

\((D_a)_{ij} = \int B_i\,x_a\,B_j\,d\mathbf r\).

Velocity

\((V_a)_{ij} = -\dfrac{i}{2}\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{m} \left(B_i\,\partial_a B_j - \partial_a B_i\,B_j\right) d\mathbf r\), the Galerkin matrix of the symmetrised operator in Eq. (31). It is \(-i/2\) times a real antisymmetric matrix, hence Hermitian.

Inverse mass

\((M_d)_{ij} = \int B_i B_j / m\,d\mathbf r\).

The velocity operator of the BenDaniel-Duke Hamiltonian is

(31)\[\hat v_a = i\,[H_0, x_a] = -\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}\,\partial_a + \partial_a\,\frac{1}{m}\right) .\]

The reported observables are multiplied by the charge: dipole \(q\langle\mathbf r\rangle\), current \(q\langle\hat{\mathbf v}\rangle\) and acceleration \(q\,d\langle\hat{\mathbf v}\rangle/dt\) (Observables).

Time Stepping

Propagator selects the integrator of Eq. (29) (Time Propagation):

  • cn (default): Crank-Nicolson [Crank1947], PETSc TSTHETA with \(\theta = 1/2\) [PETSc], applied to Eq. (30).

  • pade22: exponential midpoint rule with a Padé(2,2) approximation of the exponential.

  • magnus4: commutator-free fourth-order Magnus integrator [BlanesMoan2006] [Alvermann2011] with Padé(2,2) exponentials.
