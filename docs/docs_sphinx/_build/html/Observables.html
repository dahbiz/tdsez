Observables

During propagation a monitor evaluates the observables at \(t = 0\) and after every time step and writes them, one row per step, to td/TimeEvolutionData_<input>.h5 (HDF5 Output Schema). All quantities are in atomic units. Expectation values are formed with the coefficient vector \(\mathbf c\) of the state and the matrices of the B-spline basis; the inner product is the one of the mass (overlap) matrix,

(47)\[\langle\psi|\phi\rangle_M = \mathbf c_\psi^\dagger\,\mathbf M\,\mathbf c_\phi , \qquad \langle A\rangle = \mathbf c^\dagger\,\mathbf A\,\mathbf c ,\]

where \(\mathbf A\) is the Galerkin matrix of the operator. Expectation values are not divided by the norm, so with a CAP they decrease with the absorbed probability. PhysicsOutput (Output Control) selects which groups are computed; the others are written as zeros.

Here \(q\) = Charge and \(s = -q\) (Physics Conventions); the propagated Hamiltonian is \(H_E(t) = K + sV + s\,\mathbf E(t)\cdot\mathbf r\) (plus the CAP when enabled), with \(V\) the Potential expression.

Evaluation

The matrix-vector products of a monitor call are reduced in one fused global reduction. In 2D and 3D two further optimisations apply, each checked against the assembled matrices on the state at the first monitor call and used only if it agrees to a relative \(10^{-10}\); the matrices that are then no longer needed are freed:

  • Factorised operators. On the tensor B-spline basis \(\mathbf M\), the position matrices \(\mathbf D_a\), and for a constant mass the velocities and \(\mathbf K\), are Kronecker products of 1D banded matrices and are applied without the assembled matrix. -tdsez_tensor_ops 0 disables this.

  • Quadrature expectation values. \(\langle V\rangle\), \(\langle 1/m\rangle\), \(\langle K\rangle\) (varying mass), the CAP, the forces and the velocities are summed directly over the quadrature points of the assembly. -tdsez_quad_obs 0 disables this.

Neither is used with Dirichlet walls (BoundaryType = dirichlet) or in 1D. At start-up TDSE-Z prints which operators and expectation values use these paths and the largest relative difference found in the check.

Norm

(48)\[N(t) = \langle\psi(t)|\psi(t)\rangle_M = \mathbf c^\dagger\mathbf M\mathbf c .\]

It is the column Norm of the energies dataset.

Dipole

(49)\[D_a(t) = q\,\langle\psi(t)|\,x_a\,|\psi(t)\rangle , \qquad a \in \{x, y, z\},\]

computed as \(q\,\mathbf c^\dagger\mathbf D_a\mathbf c\) with the position matrix \((\mathbf D_a)_{ij} = \int B_i\,x_a\,B_j\). The dipoles dataset stores, next to it, the physical field \(E_a(t)\) (the LaserX/LaserY/LaserZ value on an axis in Polarization, zero otherwise) and the dipole acceleration. All spatial axes are observed by default; with ObserveAllAxes = 0 only the axes in Polarization are, and the columns of the others are zero.

Energies

The energies dataset stores

(50)\[E_{\text{kin}} = \langle K\rangle, \qquad E_{\text{pot}} = \langle sV\rangle, \qquad E_{\text{int}} = s\,\mathbf E(t)\cdot\langle\mathbf r\rangle, \qquad E_{\text{tot}} = \mathrm{Re}\,\langle H\rangle + E_{\text{int}} ,\]

where \(E_{\text{pot}}\) is the potential term as it enters the Hamiltonian (including the sign \(s\)), \(E_{\text{int}}\) the expectation value of the field coupling, and \(H\) the field-free Hamiltonian including the CAP. The CAP \(-iW\) contributes only to the imaginary part of \(\langle H\rangle\), so \(E_{\text{tot}} = E_{\text{kin}} + E_{\text{pot}} + E_{\text{int}}\). The dataset also stores

(51)\[\langle m^{-1}\rangle = \langle\psi|\,m(\mathbf r)^{-1}\,|\psi\rangle ,\]

which enters the operator form of the acceleration and indicates which mass regions the state occupies. The column layout is given in HDF5 Output Schema.

Acceleration

The dipole acceleration \(A_a = q\,\mathrm d\langle v_a\rangle/\mathrm dt = \ddot D_a\) (columns A* of dipoles) is computed in one of two forms, selected by AccelerationMethod.

operator (Ehrenfest force, acceleration form [Burnett1992]):

(52)\[A_a(t) = q\,s\left[\Big\langle -\frac{1}{m}\,\partial_a V \Big\rangle + \text{(mass-gradient terms)} - E_a(t)\,\Big\langle \frac{1}{m}\Big\rangle\right] .\]

\(\partial_a V\) is the PotentialDerivativeX/Y/Z expression (Potential & Mass), which a propagation run requires on every axis. The form is accurate for a smooth potential and mass and a wave packet that does not reach a reflecting wall.

discrete (equation of motion of the Galerkin system Eq. (40)):

(53)\[A_a(t) = -2q\,\mathrm{Im}\big(\mathbf w^\dagger\,\mathbf V_a\,\mathbf c\big), \qquad \mathbf w = \mathbf M^{-1}\big(\mathbf H_h + s\,\mathbf E(t)\cdot\mathbf D\big)\mathbf c ,\]

with the velocity matrix \(\mathbf V_a\) and \(\mathbf H_h = (\mathbf H + \mathbf H^\dagger)/2\), the Hermitian part of \(\mathbf H\). Without a CAP this is the exact time derivative of \(q\langle v_a\rangle\) for the discrete dynamics, including sharp interfaces and walls; with a CAP the absorber is excluded, which gives the Ehrenfest acceleration of the physical Hamiltonian. It costs one solve with \(\mathbf M\) per step: conjugate gradients with a block-Jacobi ILU(0) preconditioner to \(10^{-14}\) relative (about 16 iterations; options prefix accel_, e.g. -accel_ksp_type preonly -accel_pc_type lu for a direct solve). A solve that does not converge stops the run.

auto (default) uses the discrete form when the Mass expression depends on \(x\), \(y\) or \(z\) (and MassIsConstant is not set), the operator form otherwise. For smooth problems the two forms agree up to the spatial discretisation error and converge to each other with the grid (3D, position-dependent mass, degree 4: relative difference \(2.4\times10^{-4}\) at \(h_x = 1.1\), \(1.5\times10^{-5}\) at \(h_x = 0.75\)). For an eigenstate the discrete form is exactly zero and the operator form zero to the discretisation error. Once the wave packet reaches a reflecting wall they differ, because only the discrete form contains the wall force; use an absorbing potential for HHG (Simulating HHG Correctly).

The current \(J_a = \dot D_a\) and the acceleration \(A_a = \dot J_a\) give equivalent harmonic spectra,

(54)\[|\tilde A_a(\omega)|^2 = \omega^2\,|\tilde J_a(\omega)|^2 = \omega^4\,|\tilde D_a(\omega)|^2 .\]

Current

(55)\[J_a(t) = q\,\langle\psi(t)|\,v_a\,|\psi(t)\rangle = \dot D_a(t),\]

with the velocity operator \(v_a\) (\(p_a/m\) for a constant mass). The currents dataset also splits \(J_a\) with the projections of the state on the bound eigenstates \(\phi_n\) kept by the eigensolver (NBoundStates, extended to the end of a degenerate level),

(56)\[c_n(t) = \langle\phi_n|\psi(t)\rangle_M , \qquad v^{(a)}_{mn} = \langle\phi_m|\,v_a\,|\phi_n\rangle ,\]

where the matrix \(v^{(a)}_{mn}\) is computed once before the propagation:

(57)\[J^{\text{intra}}_a = q\sum_n |c_n|^2\, v^{(a)}_{nn}, \qquad J^{\text{inter}}_a = q\sum_{m\ne n} \bar c_m\, c_n\, v^{(a)}_{mn}, \qquad J^{\text{bc}}_a = J_a - J^{\text{intra}}_a - J^{\text{inter}}_a .\]

\(J^{\text{intra}}\) is the contribution of the populations, \(J^{\text{inter}}\) that of the coherences between bound states, and the remainder \(J^{\text{bc}}\) contains all terms involving the part of the state outside the span of the bound states (bound-continuum and continuum-continuum).

Angular Momentum

Column Lz of currents holds \(\langle L_z\rangle = \langle\psi|-i(x\partial_y - y\partial_x)|\psi\rangle\). The \(L_z\) matrix is assembled only in 2D with EnableLzDiag = 1; in all other cases the column is zero.

Phase Increment

Column dGamma of currents holds the phase increment of the bound-state component between consecutive steps,

(58)\[\Delta\Gamma(t) = -\arg\Big(\sum_n \bar c_n(t - h)\,c_n(t)\Big),\]

with \(c_n\) from Eq. (56) (zero at the first step). It contains the dynamical phase; the accumulated phase \(\Gamma(t) = \sum_{t' \le t}\Delta\Gamma(t')\) is obtained as the cumulative sum of the column.

Autocorrelation

(59)\[C(t) = \langle\psi(0)|\psi(t)\rangle_M = \mathbf c(0)^\dagger\,\mathbf M\,\mathbf c(t),\]

with \(\psi(0)\) the initial state. \(|C(t)|^2\) is the survival probability; the Fourier transform of \(C(t)\) gives the spectrum of the states contained in \(\psi(0)\). The autocorrelation dataset stores \(t\), \(\mathrm{Re}\,C\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\,C\).

Populations

(60)\[P_n(t) = |c_n(t)|^2 = \big|\langle\phi_n|\psi(t)\rangle_M\big|^2 , \qquad n = 0, \dots, N-1 ,\]

for the \(N\) bound eigenstates kept by the eigensolver. The populations dataset stores \(t, P_0, \dots, P_{N-1}\). The sum \(\sum_n P_n\) is the population of these bound states; for normalised states \(N(t) - \sum_n P_n\) is the population outside them (excited, unconverged and continuum states).
