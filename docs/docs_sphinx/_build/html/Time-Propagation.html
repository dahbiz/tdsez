Time Propagation

After the eigensolver has produced the initial state, TDSE-Z integrates the semi-discrete time-dependent Schrödinger equation from \(t = 0\) to FinalTime. This page describes the time parameters, the three propagators, the linear solvers they use and how to choose a time step.

Equation of Motion

The Galerkin discretisation in the B-spline basis gives

(39)\[i\,\mathbf M\,\dot{\mathbf c}(t) = \mathbf H_E(t)\,\mathbf c(t), \qquad \mathbf H_E(t) = \mathbf H + s\,\mathbf E(t)\cdot\mathbf D, \qquad s = -q ,\]

with the mass (overlap) matrix \(\mathbf M\), the field-free Hamiltonian \(\mathbf H\) (kinetic energy, potential and, with EnableCAP = 1, the complex absorbing potential, added to \(\mathbf H\) before the first step), the position matrices \(\mathbf D = (\mathbf D_x, \mathbf D_y, \mathbf D_z)\) of the axes in Polarization and the carrier charge \(q\) = Charge (Physics Conventions). Every propagator advances Eq. (39) by solving sparse systems of the form

(40)\[(\mathbf M + g\,\mathbf H_E)\,\mathbf y = \mathbf b\]

with a complex shift \(g\) proportional to the step length. All three schemes are unitary for a Hermitian \(\mathbf H_E\) (no CAP).

Propagators

Propagator selects the scheme.

cn (default)

Crank-Nicolson [Crank1947], the (1,1) Padé approximant of the step exponential, run through the PETSc TS object (TSTHETA with \(\theta = 1/2\), linear problem) [PETSc]. Second order, one solve per step with \(g = i h/2\). Standard PETSc -ts_* options apply.

pade22

Exponential midpoint rule with the (2,2) Padé approximant

(41)\[R_{22}(z) = \frac{1 + z/2 + z^2/12}{1 - z/2 + z^2/12}, \qquad z = -i h\,\mathbf M^{-1}\mathbf H_E(t + h/2) .\]

The numerator has the roots \(-3 \pm i\sqrt3\), so \(R_{22}\) factorises into two Cayley-type stages, each unitary,

(42)\[(\mathbf M + g_k\,\mathbf H_E)\,\mathbf y_k = (\mathbf M + \bar g_k\,\mathbf H_E)\,\mathbf y_{k-1}, \qquad g_{1,2} = \frac{h\,(\mp\sqrt3 + 3i)}{12}, \qquad k = 1, 2 ,\]

with \(\mathbf y_0\) the state at \(t\) and \(\mathbf y_2\) the state at \(t + h\). Two solves per step. The scheme is fourth order in the field-free phase and second order in the time dependence of the field.

magnus4

Commutator-free fourth-order Magnus integrator [BlanesMoan2006] [Alvermann2011],

(43)\[\mathbf U = \exp\!\big(h(a_1\mathbf A_1 + a_2\mathbf A_2)\big)\, \exp\!\big(h(a_2\mathbf A_1 + a_1\mathbf A_2)\big), \qquad \mathbf A_j = -i\,\mathbf M^{-1}\mathbf H_E(t + c_j h),\]

with the Gauss nodes \(c_{1,2} = 1/2 \mp \sqrt3/6\) and \(a_{1,2} = (3 \mp 2\sqrt3)/12\). Since \(a_1 + a_2 = 1/2\), each factor is a half step with an effective field \(2(a_2\mathbf E_1 + a_1\mathbf E_2)\) (applied first) and \(2(a_1\mathbf E_1 + a_2\mathbf E_2)\), evaluated with Eq. (42). Fourth order overall, four solves per step.

At start-up the rational propagators print their configuration, and at the end the number of stage solves and the mean number of GMRES iterations per solve.

Linear Solver

Every system Eq. (40) is solved with GMRES [Saad1986] (restart 30, or 50 above \(10^5\) unknowns; at most 1000 iterations) to a relative residual of \(10^{-12}\). The preconditioner is applied from the right, so the stopping test acts on the true residual of the original system whatever the preconditioner. The default preconditioner is restricted additive Schwarz [CaiSarkis1999] with one subdomain per rank and ILU(0) on each subdomain. -tdsez_ilu_levels n sets another ILU fill level; in measurements ILU(1) saved at most one iteration in 2D and none in 3D, while its factorisation was about 10 times slower in 3D (degree 4).

The preconditioner is built from the operator of the first step and reused (refresh below). The solves use a non-zero initial guess; for pade22 and magnus4 it is extrapolated from previous steps (below).

Rational propagators. The stage matrices of Eq. (42) are assembled with the following structure (Fig. 7):

  • \(\mathbf B_k = \mathbf M + g_k\mathbf H\) is cached and recomputed only when the step length changes (first step, shortened last step).

  • \(\mathbf A_k = \mathbf B_k + g_k\,s\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf D\) is formed in one pass over the stored values when all matrices share the same sparsity structure (checked once), otherwise by matrix additions.

  • Since \(\bar g_1 = -g_2\), the right-hand side of stage \(k\) is \(2\mathbf M\mathbf x - \mathbf A_{3-k}\mathbf x\).

  • Each stage solve starts from its input \(\mathbf x\) plus the stage increment \(\boldsymbol\delta = \mathbf y - \mathbf x\) extrapolated from the last \(q\) steps,

    (44)\[\mathbf y^{(n)}_{\text{init}} = \mathbf x^{(n)} + \sum_{j=1}^{q} (-1)^{j-1}\binom{q}{j}\,\boldsymbol\delta^{(n-j)} ,\]

    with \(q\) = -pade_guess_order (1 to 5, default 3); -pade_extrapolate_guess 0 starts from \(\mathbf x\) instead.

  • The two stage solvers carry the options prefixes pade_stage1_ and pade_stage2_ (for example -pade_stage1_ksp_rtol, -pade_stage2_pc_type).

Preconditioner refresh. For a strong field the operator drifts away from the one the preconditioner was built from. The first solve after each build sets a reference iteration count \(n_0\); a solve that needs more than \(2n_0 + 5\) iterations causes the preconditioner to be rebuilt from the current operator at the next solve. This applies to every propagator and preconditioner. In a 2D test with a field of 2 a.u. and \(h = 0.2\), a fixed preconditioner needed 4, 13, 24, 65, 249 and 641 iterations in successive steps and then failed; with the refresh the run converges with 4 to 8 rebuilds. cn prints the number of rebuilds at the end of the run.

Preconditioners

Preconditioner selects the preconditioner of every propagator solve. The outer solve always uses the full operator and the same tolerance, so the choice changes the cost, not the result.

asm (default)

Restricted additive Schwarz, one subdomain per rank, ILU(0) subdomain solves.

mg

Geometric multigrid on the B-spline space inside flexible GMRES (requires Basis = bspline). Each coarse level drops every other simple interior knot of each axis (repeated knots, such as C0 interfaces, are kept), as long as each axis keeps at least \(p+1\) spans and two spans per rank. The coarse spaces are therefore nested in the fine one and the prolongation is exact knot insertion, applied axis by axis as a tensor product and checked at set-up on a linear function. Coarse operators are Galerkin products \(\mathbf P^T\mathbf A\mathbf P\). Each level is smoothed by MGSmooth Richardson sweeps with ASM/ILU(0) before and after the coarse correction; the coarsest level (below 2000 DOFs, or after MGLevels levels) is solved by LU with MUMPS when PETSc provides it.

gamg

PETSc algebraic multigrid (smoothed aggregation) inside flexible GMRES. Its default Chebyshev/Jacobi smoothing needed 218 to 279 iterations on this complex operator in 2D. Use it with the ASM/ILU(0) smoother, as benchmarks/preconditioners/run.sh does: -mg_levels_ksp_type richardson -mg_levels_ksp_max_it 1 -mg_levels_pc_type asm -mg_levels_sub_pc_type ilu (with the pade_stage1_ / pade_stage2_ prefixes for the rational propagators).

PETSc options with the solver prefix (-mg_levels_*, -mg_coarse_*; -pade_stage1_*, -pade_stage2_*) override these defaults. Gauss-Seidel/SOR smoothing is not used for mg: on degree-5 splines it needed 14 to 26 iterations on one rank (60 to 80 on four) against 1 to 4 with ILU smoothing, in line with the known degree dependence of standard smoothers for B-spline discretisations.

Cost of the GMRES solves. Orthogonalisation takes 1-2 % of a solve; the rest is preconditioner applications (ILU triangular solves, about 60 %) and matrix-vector products. The cost is reduced by fewer iterations:

  • the extrapolated initial guess Eq. (44) (default \(q = 3\)) lowers the mean from 5.5 to 4.1 iterations per solve in 3D and from 5.0 to 3.4 in 2D;

  • a looser tolerance, -pade_stage1_ksp_rtol 1e-11 -pade_stage2_ksp_rtol 1e-11, saves another 10-13 % and changes the outputs by about \(5\times10^{-11}\).

Pipelined GMRES (-pade_stage1_ksp_type pgmres, likewise for stage 2) overlaps the global reduction of each iteration with the next product. On one machine a reduction costs microseconds through shared memory and the extra vector work dominates: time stepping was 20-35 % slower (3D, 8 ranks, same 4.1 iterations per solve). It may pay off across many nodes, where reductions cross the network. It does not remove load imbalance (MPI Notes).

Strong fields. With \(\tau = |g|\) the operator is \(\mathbf M + i\tau(\mathbf H + s\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf r)\), where \(\tau = h/2\) for cn, \(h/\sqrt{12}\) for pade22 and \(h/(2\sqrt{12})\) for magnus4. When \(\tau\max|\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf r|\) exceeds about 1.5 the field term dominates and the operator behaves like an indefinite Helmholtz operator that the coarse levels of multigrid cannot represent: in 2D tests mg needed 3-18 iterations up to 1.5 and diverged at 3, where asm still converged (5-33 iterations). With Preconditioner = mg TDSE-Z estimates \(\tau\max|\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf r|\) over the pulse and the box at set-up and prints a warning above 1.5. Typical strong-field runs (\(E \le 0.2\) a.u., \(h \le 0.05\), box \(\le 100\) a.u.) stay well below this value.

Choosing a Time Step

For an eigencomponent of energy \(E\) the exact step multiplies the coefficient by \(e^{-i\theta}\), \(\theta = Eh\). The rational approximants reproduce the modulus exactly and the phase approximately:

(45)\[\varphi_{\text{cn}} = 2\arctan\frac{\theta}{2} = \theta\Big(1 - \frac{\theta^2}{12} + O(\theta^4)\Big), \qquad \varphi_{22} = 2\arctan\frac{\theta/2}{1 - \theta^2/12} = \theta\Big(1 - \frac{\theta^4}{720} + O(\theta^6)\Big).\]

Crank-Nicolson also scales the group velocity of a component by \(1/(1 + \theta^2/4)\), which slows fast electrons; the Padé(2,2) propagators remove this error to fourth order (Fig. 8).

In practice:

  • choose \(h\) so that \(E_{\max} h\) is small for the highest energy that matters (the cut-off of the photoelectron or harmonic spectrum), and so that the field period and envelope are well resolved;

  • with pade22 or magnus4 a much larger step reaches the same accuracy: on the free-packet t-SURFF benchmark (Photoelectron Spectra (t-SURFF), \(T = 40\)) pade22 with \(h = 0.025\) reaches a spectrum error of \(1.2\times10^{-6}\), against \(3.3\times10^{-4}\) for cn with \(h = 0.0125\);

  • magnus4 is fourth order also in the time dependence of the field, at twice the cost per step of pade22;

  • confirm convergence by repeating the run with \(h/2\) and comparing the observables of interest.

Propagation Control and Progress

With EnablePropagation = 0 the run ends after the eigensolver, which is useful to check eigenvalues and eigenstates (Eigensolver) before a long propagation. With EnablePropagation = 1 the propagation starts from the state selected by InitialState (Initial State).

The monitor prints a progress line with the step, the elapsed fraction, the time, the squared norm \(\langle\psi|\mathbf M|\psi\rangle\) and the total energy (Observables):

[    10/  2000]    0.5%  t=    0.1000   <Psi|Psi>=   1.000e+00   <H>= -5.0000e-01

With Verbose = 1 (default) a line is printed every step; with Verbose = 0 about twenty lines are printed over the run.

Without a CAP the norm is conserved to the solver tolerance by all three propagators. With EnableCAP = 1 it decreases as the CAP absorbs outgoing flux; the decrease is the absorbed probability.

Troubleshooting

A stage solve does not converge

  • The error Pade(2,2) stage k solve did not converge stops the run.

  • Check whether the field is strong for the chosen step (\(\tau\max|\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf r| > 1.5\), see above); reduce TimeStep or use Preconditioner = asm.

Norm not conserved without a CAP

  • Check that EnableCAP = 0.

  • Check the GMRES convergence with -pade_stage1_ksp_converged_reason (-ksp_converged_reason for cn).

Missing or zero time series

  • Check PhysicsOutput (Output Control): quantities not listed are written as zeros.
